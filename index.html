Changing demands, context-dependent weights

As robots encounter more environments and operations, a shared policy must accommodate increasingly diverse demands. Even placing an object in a tray involves reaching, contact, transport, and precise placement under one instruction.

Conventional VLA policies change their activations and actions with context, while their effective weights stay fixed during execution. Different operations can favor competing parameter updates, creating trade-offs when trained together.

MorphAct lets the policy’s effective weights change with the current interaction. It learns a shared rule that generates context-dependent updates for the vision–language and action modules, enabling specialization within one policy.

Y^t=πθ+Δθ(xt)(xt)

xt = (ot, ℓ, st) is the current context; Δθ(xt) is its generated update; Ŷt is the predicted action chunk.

Where shared parameters face competing demands

Operation-level diagnostics on 65 atomic tasks in RoboCasa365 examine the demands placed on shared parameters. At one checkpoint, gradient alignment, one-step transfer, and parameter-group contributions reveal local adaptation trade-offs in both the vision–language and action modules.

A positive transfer score reduces the target loss; a negative score increases it. Every source update starts from the same checkpoint.

All 260 measured source-improving updates increase loss on other sampled tasks. Opposing parameter-group contributions can also cancel within a module. These local trade-offs motivate a shared parameter-generation mechanism whose outputs vary with the interaction context.

Where conflict concentrates

The action groups outside Transformer blocks, block 00, and block 11 account for 78.88% of pooled negative contribution before rounding. Together, these groups contribute most of the measured negative mass on the action side.

How it varies with depth

Morph from the environment, then act

MorphAct first generates vision–language updates from observation and instruction embeddings. The adapted VLM pass produces features that, together with robot state, condition action-side parameter generation. The two generators are coupled through the adapted representation used for control.

Both generators learn jointly through the native action objective, without operation labels or expert-adapter targets. Low-rank factors implement these contextual updates efficiently; the generated factors are reused throughout each action chunk.

Coupled parameter generation

Δθv,t=Gv(et;φv)(ht,Ct)=Fv(et;θv+Δθv,t)
ua,t=Ca(ht,st;ρa)Δθa,t=Ga(ua,t;φa)

Both ht and Ct come from the adapted VLM pass: ht conditions the action generator, while Ct follows the native action-conditioning path.

ΔWj,t=αjrjAj,tBj,t

For block j, A: din × r and B: r × dout. The base weights stay frozen; the low-rank factors change with the context.

Φ includes both generators, the state projection, and projectors retained as trainable by the adaptation configuration. ξ is the randomness used by the native objective.

The action generator uses final adapted multimodal tokens and robot state. Layer-wise prefix key–value states provide native action conditioning. Noisy actions and solver time remain in the native action path.

Consistent gains across VLA architectures

Across X-VLA, π0, and π0.5, MorphAct improves average success on both LIBERO and Meta-World. Meta-World gains range from 7.57 to 16.30 percentage points, supporting contextual parameterization across different VLA architectures.

Why context and adapted features matter

Six X-VLA variants on LIBERO isolate three choices: the generation condition, the modules receiving updates, and the features connecting the generators.

MorphActAblated variant

Context beyond task identity. Current-context generation reaches 97.20% mean success, versus 88.25% for Task-ID and 87.50% for a constant condition—a +8.95% gain over Task-ID.

Adapt both modules. Action-only reaches 81.00%; VLM-only, 43.50%. Adding upstream adaptation raises Action-only to 86.00%, even before coupling.

Couple through adapted features. Mean success rises from 86.00% to 97.20% (+11.20%). On Long, it rises from 71.60% to 93.20% (+21.60%).
